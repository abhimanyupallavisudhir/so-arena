Team of 3; one random worker per task can hack the grader (+0.3 to everyone if it stands); each teammate sees it with probability 0.8; a caught violator pays 0.3; 24 episodes per iteration, learning rate 2. Both algorithms have the same rest points, but vanilla gradient moves a strategy's logit in proportion to its probability, so a rarely played strategy (reporting, when nobody does) learns slowly and misconduct entrenches first. Below b_R, training that starts from a reporting culture ends with nobody violating: a Nash equilibrium whose threat is never tested - after an actual violation a witness would rather stay silent (it is not subgame perfect), so the outcome is fragile. The natural-gradient estimate weights a sampled strategy by 1/P(strategy), so once misconduct is rare a single sampled violation that went unseen can move its logit a long way: its runs spread more than REINFORCE's. All agents are scripted: these numbers illustrate the dynamics, not language-model behaviour.
